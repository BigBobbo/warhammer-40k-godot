# The 2000-point fixtures and their structural bias F

**Date:** 2026-08-08 · **Built by:** `40k/tests/make_2000pt_fixture.gd` ·
**Code:** `d122f2d` (measured from a snapshot tree, so the season records
carry `git=unknown`; the tree is `git archive` of that sha plus nothing)

## Why these replaced the 1335/1840-pt fixtures

Every number this lab produced before today came from armies spliced out of
`audit_baseline_postdeploy`: Custodes at **1335** points, Orks at **1840**.
40k is balanced at 2000 and that is what this game is designed to be played
at, so those numbers described a format nobody fields. Neither army matched
any file in `40k/armies/` either — they were not lists a player can pick.

These are built from the **shipped** lists through the game's own
`ArmyListManager.load_army_list` + `apply_army_to_game_state`, so display
names, ability canonicalisation, wargear and enhancement stat bonuses,
model-profile wounds, model-count correction and the Custodes deep-strike
backfill all happen exactly as they do for a player:

| | list | faction / detachment | units | models | points |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Custodes | `custodes_lions` | Adeptus Custodes / Lions of the Emperor | 11 | 42 | 2000 |
| Orks | `recon_stomps` | Orks / Speedwaaagh! | 17 | 77 | 2000 |

Six fixtures: `mirror_custodes_2000_*`, `mirror_orks_2000_*` and
`asym_2000_*`, each in a `_postdeploy` (round 1 Command, both armies packed
into their zones, P2 the exact 180° rotation of P1) and a `_predeploy`
(`Phase.DEPLOYMENT`, nothing on the table) variant. All six pass
`tools/ai_lab/fixture_check.py`.

## What F is, and why it is not a defect

Same AI, shipped defaults, both sides (A/A), Hard (difficulty 2). The mean
margin P2−P1 is then the fixture's **structural bias** — first turn plus board
side — and nothing else. `run_paired.py` side-swaps every seed, so F cancels
exactly and a reported effect E is free of it. A large, *known*, *stable* F is
fine; an unknown or drifting one is not. That is the only reason this table
exists.

## Reading it

**`mirror_custodes_2000_postdeploy`, F = −3.60 ± 2.54.** The 95% interval
[−8.57, +1.37] straddles zero, so F is not distinguishable from zero at 20
games — which is what a mirror *should* look like once first-turn advantage is
the only asymmetry and the board is rotationally symmetric. The point estimate
is not zero, and with 20 games it cannot be shown to be: an interval this wide
would fail to detect a real 3-VP bias. Treat "F is small" as established and
"F is exactly zero" as untested.

**Spread is the number that actually governs cost.** sd 11.35 is squarely in
the 9-15 VP band the old fixtures showed, so moving to 2000 points did *not*
make per-seed margins noisier — but each game now costs **162 s median**
against ~48 s on the retired 1335-pt Custodes mirror. Same games needed,
3.4x the wall clock. Budget for a paired A/B accordingly: 12 pairs = 24 games
is about 65 minutes at 2 lanes, not 20.

**Zero stalls, zero timeouts across 20 games.** That is the stall-rate
reference future soaks are read against, and it is worth stating explicitly
for a fixture with 84 models on the table — the packing is legal and the game
does not wedge on it.

**`mirror_custodes_2000_predeploy`, F = −0.25 ± 5.83 — and this is the row
that should change how you plan.** F itself is the least interesting part: it
is indistinguishable from zero, as a mirror should be. The number that matters
is **sd 20.21 against the post-deployment fixture's 11.35**. Letting the AI
place its own army nearly doubles per-seed spread, and games needed for a given
standard error scale with sd²:

| | post-deployment | pre-deployment | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| sd (VP/game) | 11.35 | 20.21 | 1.78x |
| s/game | 162 | 227 | 1.40x |
| games for equal SE | *n* | 3.17 *n* | **3.17x** |
| **wall clock for equal statistical power** | *t* | | **4.44x** |

So a paired A/B that resolves in an hour on `_postdeploy` needs roughly
**four and a half hours** on `_predeploy` to reach the same confidence. That is
the real price of covering deployment, and it is a much bigger number than the
1.4x per-game cost suggests on its own.

It is also the justification for keeping both, stated as a measurement rather
than a preference:

* Testing a **shooting, charge or fight** change on `_predeploy` pays 4.4x for
  variance from a phase the change cannot touch. Use `_postdeploy`. That is
  variance reduction, not a shortcut.
* Testing anything that reads a `DEPLOY_*` parameter — or asking "is the AI
  better at the game a player plays?" — has no alternative. Use `_predeploy`,
  and budget for 4.4x.

Both fixtures completed every game with zero stalls and zero timeouts,
including deployment of all 84 models.

**`asym_2000_postdeploy`, F = −24.00 ± 3.66.** Large, negative and clearly
non-zero: P1 (Custodes, 2000 pts) beats P2 (Orks, 2000 pts) by 24 VP a game
with both sides on identical defaults. Unlike the retired asymmetric fixture
this is **not** a points difference — both armies are 2000 — so it is first
turn plus whatever edge 42 elite bodies have over 77 Ork ones at holding five
objectives on this board.

That is fine and must not be "fixed": `run_paired` side-swaps every seed, so F
cancels exactly and the reported effect E is free of it. A large *known*,
*stable* F is fine; an unknown or drifting one is not, which is the entire
reason for writing it down with its interval. What it does mean is that this
fixture must never be read on its own — a raw margin here says nothing without
the side swap.

Its spread (sd 11.57) matches the Custodes mirror's 11.35 almost exactly, so it
costs no more games per VP of resolution; it is simply slower per game (300 s
against 162 s).

**Comparison to the fixture it replaces.** The 1335-pt Custodes mirror measured
F = −0.25 ± 2.67 over 20 games (`2026-08_frozen_baseline.md`). −3.60 ± 2.54 is
not significantly different from that (the intervals overlap heavily), so
nothing here suggests the 2000-pt mirror is structurally worse-behaved; it is
the same kind of fixture, four times more expensive, on armies people play.

## Reproducing

```bash
python3 tools/ai_lab/run_lanes.py --fixture mirror_custodes_2000_postdeploy \
    --seeds 7001-7020 --arm AA --lanes 2 --difficulty 2 --time-scale 6 \
    --season bench_data/aa_cust2000_post
```

Run these from a snapshot of the tree, not the working tree: editing
`AIDecisionMaker.gd` while a season is queued has already corrupted one run
and cost ~30 games.
