# New AI vs old AI — mirror match, 2026-08-06

**Question:** do the YouTube-corpus findings (commit `af999b8`) make the AI win
more games?

**Answer: no. No improvement is demonstrated, and the point estimate slightly
favours the OLD behaviour.**

## Why this fixture and not `audit_baseline_postdeploy`

The standing benchmark fixture turned out to be corrupt — see commit `ef8abff`.
It carries an army-list header row imported as a unit (`U_STRIKE_FORCE_A`,
`keywords ["UNKNOWN"]`, `points 2000`), which inflated the Ork army total from
1840 to 3840 pts, doubled the 11e 20.01 Strategic Reserves allowance from 920
to 1920, and let the AI put **69% of its army in reserve**. That fixture starts
the Orks with 570 pts and 7 models on the board against 1120 pts and 17 models
of Custodes, which is the real content of its "1000 pts beats 2000 pts by
44 VP" headline.

`mirror_orks_postdeploy` (generated by `tests/make_mirror_fixture.py`) is the
same board with an identical 1840-pt Ork army on both sides, no placeholder
unit, and every model deployed — so there is no army asymmetry, no points
asymmetry and no reserve-arrival confound left to reason about.

Balance of the instrument, one game at Hard each:

| fixture | margin (P2−P1) |
|---|---|
| `audit_baseline_postdeploy` | −44 |
| mirror, placeholder still present | +3 |
| **`mirror_orks_postdeploy` (clean)** | **−2** |

## Design

`margin = VP(P2) − VP(P1)`; `F` = structural bias (first turn + board side);
`E` = new-minus-old.

| arm | P1 | P2 | measures | seeds |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **AA** | new | new | `F` — the A/A null | 7001–7010 |
| **M1** | old | new | `F + E` | 7001–7010 |
| **M2** | new | old | `F − E` | 7001–7010 |

`E = (M1 − M2)/2` cancels `F`; `F = (M1 + M2)/2` must agree with the AA arm or
the harness is biased. Hard (difficulty 2), `BENCH_TIME_SCALE=6`.

```bash
for arm in "on on" "off on" "on off"; do set -- $arm; \
  BENCH_SEED_BASE=7000 BENCH_DIFFICULTY=2 BENCH_TIME_SCALE=6 \
  bash 40k/tests/run_ai_benchmark.sh 10 mirror_orks_postdeploy \
    res://tests/bench_profiles/yt_findings_$1.json \
    res://tests/bench_profiles/yt_findings_$2.json; done
```

## Conclusions

1. **No improvement.** Two independent estimates now straddle zero: +3.0 VP on
   the asymmetric fixture (13 paired seeds) and **−2.05 VP** here. Neither is
   significant. The head-to-head record (6–12) leans against the new
   behaviour without resolving it either. Nothing in this evidence supports
   shipping the behavioural weights as tuned values.

2. **The loss, such as it is, is on secondaries** (−1.6 of the −2.1). That is
   the shape you would expect if the AI is being made more cautious — holding
   fire to stay Hidden and paying an Overwatch tax to cross open ground both
   trade tempo for safety, and secondaries in 11e mostly pay for doing things.
   Suggestive, not established; an ablation per finding would be needed to
   attribute it.

3. **First-turn advantage on a clean board is small.** The AA arm shows
   P1 38.1 vs P2 34.2 VP (margin −3.90 ± 4.89), nowhere near the ~19 VP that
   `2026-07-10_firstturn_swap.md` measured at Hard. That earlier figure was
   very likely an artifact of the same reserves bug: going second while 69% of
   your army is off the table is far worse than going second.

4. **Prior tuning is suspect.** Every AI baseline in this repo was measured on
   the corrupt fixture, including the `FACTION_AGGRESSION_ORKS 1.8 → 1.2`
   change, which was justified by Ork primary VP "starving at ~6/game" — the
   expected behaviour of an army fielding 31% of itself. Re-testing that
   decision on this fixture is worth doing before any further tuning.

5. **Mirror alone is not enough.** A change that helps in an Ork mirror may
   hurt elsewhere. `make_mirror_fixture.py --source 1` builds the Custodes
   mirror; a two-matchup grid is the minimum guard against overfitting, and
   the asymmetric matchup should be re-run once a *correct* version of it
   exists.

## Recommendation

Split the change by evidence class:

* **Keep unconditionally** — bug fixes that need no win-rate evidence: the
  deep-strike denial radius 9"→8" (the engine already implements 8"), the
  reinforcement validator's matching 9"→8", not paying the objective-holder
  premium for an already-battle-shocked target, and the placeholder-unit guard.
* **Do not ship as defaults on this evidence** — the five behavioural weights
  (`WEIGHT_HIDDEN_GAINED`, `HIDDEN_FORFEIT_PENALTY`, `HIDDEN_WINDOW_BONUS`,
  `OVERWATCH_EXPOSURE_PENALTY`, `BATTLE_SHOCK_DENIAL_WEIGHT`). They are
  corpus-suggested starting points that have never been tuned, and the only
  two measurements of them straddle zero. Defaulting them to neutral keeps the
  machinery and the tests while leaving `yt_findings_on.json` as the opt-in
  arm for a proper tuning sweep.

---

## Decision taken (2026-08-06)

The recommendation above was applied. In `AIDecisionMaker.gd` the five
behavioural weights now default to neutral:

| constant | shipped | corpus-suggested |
|---|---|---|
| `WEIGHT_HIDDEN_GAINED` | 0.0 | 4.0 |
| `HIDDEN_FORFEIT_PENALTY` | 0.0 | 3.0 |
| `HIDDEN_WINDOW_BONUS` | 1.0 | 1.25 |
| `OVERWATCH_EXPOSURE_PENALTY` | 0.0 | 0.35 |
| `BATTLE_SHOCK_DENIAL_WEIGHT` | 0.0 | 1.0 |

The bug fixes ship ON and are unaffected: the deep-strike denial radius derived
at 8", the matching fix to `_is_valid_reinforcement_position`, not paying the
objective-holder premium for an already-battle-shocked target, and the
placeholder-unit guard. None of those are behaviour tuning; they are the AI
being brought into line with rules the engine already implements.

`tests/scenarios/sp/ai_hidden_awareness_11e.json` now loads
`yt_findings_on.json` to exercise the dormant machinery, and asserts in both
directions: with the profile the decision is SKIP_UNIT, with it cleared (i.e.
shipped defaults) the same decision is SHOOT. 25/25 passing. That test is what
keeps the feature from bit-rotting while it is switched off.

**To revisit:** run a Custodes mirror (`make_mirror_fixture.py --source 1`)
alongside the Ork one before turning any of these back on. A change that helps
one mirror and hurts the other is the overfitting case this whole exercise
exists to catch, and only the Ork mirror has been tested.
