# research:llm-game-ai

## Narrative

SCOPE: 2023–2026 literature on LLMs inside game-playing AI, assessed against this project's specific constraints (4 cores, 1–8 min/game, VP-margin sd 11–20, deterministic seed-paired lab, ~800 actions/game, refusal to cheat on dice).

== 1. LLMs playing strategy games directly: consistently mediocre, and weakest exactly where 40k is hard ==

Chess is the cleanest measurement. Community leaderboards (maxim-saplin, dubesor) put 2024-era LLMs around 1000–1800 Elo with persistent illegal-move generation; GPT-4o's effective rating was dragged down by illegal moves, and only 2025 reasoning models (o1/o3-class) reliably produce legal, competent play — still far below engines. The one "grandmaster-level LLM chess" result (DeepMind, arXiv 2412.12119) required a transformer pre-trained on millions of engine-annotated positions plus explicit external/internal search — i.e., the classic search+learned-eval recipe, not an off-the-shelf LLM.

Cicero (Diplomacy, Science 2022) is routinely miscited as an LLM success. Its strategy came from an RL-trained planning engine (piKL self-play); the 2.7B BART-like language model, fine-tuned on 40k human games, was the *communication interface*, controlled by the planner. It scored 2x the human average and top-10% over 40 blitz-league games. No pure-LLM Diplomacy agent has matched this (reported; I found no such result in 2023–2026 literature).

CivRealm (ICLR 2024): LLM agents (BaseLang, and the hierarchical Mastaba) play full Freeciv; Mastaba clearly beats flat BaseLang (settled by turn 113 what BaseLang reached by 210), but the authors themselves say results "expose weaknesses in LLM grounding" — absolute performance is poor. TextStarCraft2 (arXiv 2312.11865): GPT-based agent with Chain-of-Summarization reached ~50% win rate vs the *built-in* Harder (Lv5) AI; 2025 prompt engineering (HEP, arXiv 2502.11122) pushed to 75% vs Harder/VeryHard and a first Elite-AI win. Critical context: SC2's built-in scripted AI is a weak baseline that competent humans beat trivially, and these agents act at macro level with scripted micro. Poker: GTO Wizard's 2026 benchmark ran every major LLM for 5,000 HU NLHE hands each; ALL lost heavily — best luck-adjusted result was −16 bb/100 (GPT-5.3), i.e., losing at 4x the rate an elite pro *wins*; their diagnosis: LLMs cannot maintain balanced ranges or long-horizon consistency under hidden information. Werewolf (ICML 2024, arXiv 2310.18940): "human-level" play was achieved by an LLM generating candidate actions with an *RL policy choosing among them* — again the decision quality comes from RL.

Agentic benchmarks quantify the ceiling. BALROG (ICLR 2025): best model (Claude 3.5 Sonnet) averaged 32.6% progression across games; NetHack ≤1.5% for every model; famous knowing-doing gap (GPT-4o eats rotten food it correctly identifies as dangerous when asked). Several VLMs got *worse* when given images instead of text. lmgame-Bench (2025): most models near-random on Sokoban/Tetris/etc. without a supporting harness; authors name brittle vision, prompt sensitivity, and data contamination as core problems. TextArena: humans still outrank top LLMs at negotiation/deception games on TrueSkill. Pokémon: Gemini 2.5 Pro "beat Pokémon Blue" in 813 hours via a heavily engineered third-party harness with minimap/pathfinding tools and developer interventions (TechCrunch's headline: "with a little help"); LessWrong analyses of Claude/Gemini runs identify vision and spatial reasoning as the dominant failure mode, largely bypassed by scaffolding, not solved.

Pattern: LLM failures cluster on (i) continuous/gridded spatial reasoning, (ii) precise arithmetic and probability, (iii) rule-legality, (iv) long-horizon consistency. Those are precisely the load-bearing skills for 40k movement, charge math, and per-model placement — the area where this project already knows ~50% of its movement decisions are bad. There is no evidence LLMs would fix that layer, and strong evidence they'd be worse than the existing heuristic engine.

== 2. LLM-as-strategist over a scripted executor: real architecture, unproven lift over a GOOD scripted layer ==

The hierarchical pattern (LLM picks macro plan, scripted/RL layer executes) is the only pattern that works at all in complex games: Mastaba > BaseLang, HEP's hierarchical prompts, TextSC2's macro-action interface, and Cicero's planner/dialogue split all confirm it. But the honest question is lift *over the scripted layer alone*, and the best direct measurement I found is damning: "Hierarchical Control in Multi-Agent Games: LLM-based Planning and RL Execution" (arXiv 2606.20014) evaluated an LLM strategic controller over RL skills in a 2v2 King-of-the-Hill game and found it statistically equivalent to hand-crafted behavior trees (46.4% vs 51.5% win rate); the measured benefit was *perceived human-likeness* (60% of study participants), not strength. TextSC2-family results beat only the weak built-in AI, an opponent class analogous to a naive scripted 40k bot — which this project's 23.5k-line engine already surpasses. I found no published case of an LLM strategist measurably outperforming a competently tuned domain-specific scripted planner in a spatial wargame. That claim would be an act of faith.

== 3. Offline LLM-as-coder/optimizer: the one literature branch with real, production-grade wins — under conditions this project mostly lacks ==

FunSearch (Nature 2024) evolved bin-packing/cap-set heuristics beating classical baselines. Eureka (ICLR 2024) generated reward functions outperforming expert human rewards across 29 Isaac Gym + 20 dexterity tasks. AlphaEvolve (DeepMind 2025) is the strongest evidence anywhere that "LLM proposes code, harness verifies" produces real value: a Borg scheduling heuristic in production recovering ~0.7% of Google's global compute, a 48-multiplication 4x4 complex matrix algorithm (first improvement over Strassen in that setting in 56 years), 23% Pallas kernel speedup. Academic descendants (EoH ICML 2024, ReEvo NeurIPS 2024, HSEvo/MEoH 2025) show LLM-evolved heuristics matching or beating hand-designed ones across combinatorial optimization.

Three critical caveats. (1) Every success rests on a cheap, fast, low-noise evaluator: bin-packing evaluation is milliseconds; Eureka leaned on massively GPU-parallel simulation; EoH still used ~2,000 LLM queries per problem. This project's evaluator is 1–8 minutes per game with sd 11–20 VP, needing hundreds of paired games to resolve +4 VP — three to five orders of magnitude more expensive per candidate. The binding constraint on this project is evaluation throughput, not idea generation, and LLMs do nothing to relax it. (2) Generalization is shaky: "Beyond the Hype: Benchmarking LLM-Evolved Heuristics for Bin Packing" (arXiv 2501.11411 — I read the abstract directly) found most FunSearch/EoH-style evolved heuristics do NOT generalize across a broad benchmark space; they are specialists whose discovery cost must be weighed against narrow applicability. (3) The project's own data is a preview: CEM over 264 games found nothing shippable (+2.25±3.2 VP), and 6 implemented findings mined from 1,029 strategy transcripts measured −2.05 VP. Plausible-sounding proposals — whether from humans, YouTube, or an LLM — mostly measure zero in this game. An LLM raises proposal *volume and articulacy*, not proposal *hit rate*, unless aimed at the demonstrated high-effect class: discrete strategic/rule-level changes like the reserves-cap fix (12→39 VP), which are detectable in ~30–60 paired games instead of hundreds.

== 4. Practicalities for a Godot indie game ==

Per-action LLM calls are ruled out on arithmetic alone. ~800 actions/game × ~1–2K tokens/call ≈ 1–2M tokens/game: on Claude Haiku 4.5 ($1/$5 per MTok, verified current pricing) that's roughly $2–4 per game and, at 1–3s+ per call, +15–40 minutes of pure API latency per game. For the lab, one 400-game A/B becomes ~$1,000+ and breaks seed-determinism. Local models on the project's 4-core CPU: a 7–8B quantized model generates perhaps 5–15 tok/s with slow CPU prompt ingestion — ~30–120s per decision (my inference from standard llama.cpp figures; not measured on this box) — unusable in-game and poisonous to the eval budget. Shipping-wise, an indie Godot game requiring either a user API key or a multi-GB model download is a product-killer. The standard mitigation, used by every system in §1–2, is coarse granularity: once per turn (~10–25 calls/game, ~$0.05–0.25 on Haiku — affordable but still non-deterministic and seconds-slow), once per game (pre-game plan), or offline only (zero runtime cost, zero determinism impact). Batch API (50% discount) helps offline synthesis only.

== 5. Failure modes vs this project's lab ==

Prompt brittleness and contamination (lmgame) mean any in-game LLM behavior is fragile across model versions; API model deprecation cycles (~12–18 months) would silently change our AI's play — fatal for a deterministic, regression-tested engine. Sampling non-determinism (even temperature-0 is not bit-stable across providers/versions) breaks the seed-paired evaluator if the LLM is in the loop *during* evaluation. Hallucinated illegality (chess) requires clamping every LLM output through RulesEngine legality anyway. The mirror-image good news: the literature's success stories (Eureka, FunSearch, AlphaEvolve) are all architecturally "LLM proposes, rigorous automated evaluator disposes" — this project's paired side-swapped evaluator + Parquet decision records + rule DSL is exactly the right verification substrate, and having it puts the project ahead of most hobby efforts. The lab keeps LLM non-determinism entirely outside the measured artifact: the LLM writes a deterministic rule/profile/plan; the engine A/B tests it as normal. Cost control requires racing/successive halving (kill candidates at 30–60 games unless a large effect shows) and per-decision proxy metrics from the existing decision logs before spending full-game budget.

== 6. Ranked verdict ==

(a) Offline rule/parameter/heuristic synthesis into the DSL, gated by the paired evaluator — RANK 1, the only integration point with genuine literature support (Eureka/FunSearch/AlphaEvolve/EoH). Expected value: modest and honest — the evaluator bottleneck and the project's own 0-for-many hit rate mean budgeting for mostly nulls; aim proposals at discrete strategic mechanisms (reserves, deployment doctrine, objective assignment policies), not damage-math or weight nudges, which are measured no-ops here. (b) Pre-game cached plan generation (deployment templates, per-mission/army earmarked game plans, target-priority tables) — RANK 2, speculative: no published lift numbers exist for "LLM writes the opening book," but it is structurally sound (offline, deterministic, zero runtime cost, human/eval-vettable) and targets the project's measured highest-leverage phase (deployment decides rounds 1–2; reserves change was the one big win). This is really a special case of (a). (c) Once-per-turn in-game strategic re-planning — RANK 3, low EV: the supporting evidence is "beats SC2's weak built-in AI" and "statistically equivalent to behavior trees"; costs determinism, latency, money, and shipping simplicity for no demonstrated strength gain over a tuned phase-planner. (d) Per-action decisions — RANK 4, ruled out by cost/latency arithmetic, spatial-reasoning failure evidence (Pokémon, BALROG, chess legality), and the deterministic-lab requirement. Bottom line: LLMs are a legitimate *offline hypothesis generator and code assistant* for this project, not a performance engine; the hope that "LLMs at various stages give us more performance" is supported only at the offline stage, weakly, and the fundamental feasibility question (can heuristics+limited search make a challenging spatial wargame AI) is neither answered nor materially changed by anything in the LLM literature.

  Evidence: EoH: 2,000 LLM queries per problem; FunSearch: millions of samples; Eureka: 16 reward samples x 5 runs each requiring full RL training runs on GPU-parallel Isaac Gym; project's own stated eval costs

  Evidence: Verified current Anthropic pricing (Haiku 4.5 $1/$5 per MTok, batch 50% off); arithmetic on the project's measured 800 actions/game; local-inference throughput figures are standard llama.cpp numbers, not measured on this hardware

## Implications

Concretely for this project: (1) Do NOT put an LLM in the per-action or per-turn decision loop — the literature shows LLMs are weakest at exactly the continuous-spatial, arithmetic, and legality skills the 40k action layer needs, the best measured LLM-strategist result is parity with behavior trees, and 800 calls/game destroys cost, latency, determinism, and shippability. (2) The only evidence-backed integration is offline: use an LLM (e.g., via the Batch API at 50% discount, or interactive Claude sessions) as a hypothesis generator that writes candidate rules/profiles/plans into the existing rule DSL, then gate every candidate through the existing seed-paired evaluator — this mirrors the Eureka/FunSearch/AlphaEvolve architecture exactly, and the lab is genuinely the right verification substrate. (3) Respect the evaluator bottleneck: adopt racing/successive-halving (kill candidates at 30-60 paired games unless a large effect appears), use the Parquet per-decision logs as cheap proxy screens before spending full-game budget, and aim LLM proposals exclusively at the discrete-strategic class where the project's one big win lives (deployment doctrine, reserves policy, objective assignment, charge-commitment rules) — not at damage math or weight tuning, which the project has measured to be no-ops. (4) The most promising cheap experiment: LLM-drafted, human-reviewed deployment templates and per-mission/army pre-game plans, cached as data, A/B tested with side-swapped pairing — this targets the measured highest-leverage phase at zero runtime cost; treat it as speculative (no published lift numbers exist for this pattern) and let the lab decide. (5) Budget expectations honestly: the project's own -2.05 VP transcript-mining result suggests most LLM proposals will measure zero; plan for many nulls, and if a few months of offline-synthesis cycles produce no second reserves-cap-class win, that is a legitimate 'not worth pursuing further' stopping point. LLMs do not change the fundamental feasibility question — they only make hypothesis generation cheaper, not measurement.
