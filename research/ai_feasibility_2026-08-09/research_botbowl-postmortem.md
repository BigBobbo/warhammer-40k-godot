# research:botbowl-postmortem

## Narrative

## What Blood Bowl's decision space actually is (verified from Justesen et al., CoG 2019)

Blood Bowl is fully observable, stochastic, turn-based, on a 26x15 grid, 11 pieces per side, 32 total coach turns (16 each), ~1600 atomic steps per game. The step-wise action space varies from 1 to 395 choices; each piece can be moved multiple squares per turn, making it a "nested multi-action game." The paper's analytic estimate of the turn-wise branching factor is 10^50; empirically measured, it is ~10^30 under random play (29.8 step branching, 10.6 actions/turn) and 17.2^41.6 ≈ 4.8x10^51 under GrodBot play. The defining rule is the TURNOVER: any failed dice roll (fall, failed pickup, failed catch) ends your whole turn immediately. Scoring is brutally sparse: in 350,000 random-vs-random games, ZERO touchdowns were scored — every game was a 0-0 draw. This one fact drives almost everything that followed: vanilla RL and MCTS have no gradient to climb, and "safe actions first, risky last" risk-sequencing is the core human skill.

## Competition results, year by year (verified from official Bot Bowl pages)

**Bot Bowl I (2019), 5 agents.** Round-robin W/D/L: GrodBot (scripted) 34/5/1; EvoGrod (GrodBot with 16 heuristic params evolved) 31/5/4; NO ONE (A2C+self-play RL) 8/12/20; A2C (RL, reward shaping) 0/17/23; Random 0/15/25. GrodBot beat EvoGrod in the final 3 wins to 0 (one tie). Two verified takeaways: the pure-RL entrant A2C finished statistically indistinguishable from the random baseline (0 wins each), and evolutionary tuning of a hand-tuned scripted bot's parameters made it WORSE than the hand-tuned original. Organizers flagged scripted-bot weaknesses: drive setups, blocking strategy, "long term planning."

**Bot Bowl II (2020), 7 agents.** Minigrod (scripted) 47/10/3 won; GrodBot MKII (scripted) 47/7/6; Scripted Plus Bot 41/7/12; Gotebot (A2C + self-play + curriculum, plus two scripted rules) 26/9/25 — the only ML entrant above random; Sapling (MCTS + scripted heuristic) 1/20/39; Random 0/23/37; Giaobot (IMPALA + LSTM + self-play) 0/20/40 — an RL bot that finished BELOW the random baseline, with 0 TDs in 60 games. Pezzotti's later paper states the best ML bot of this era (Gotebot/GbgBot) "could win only 70% of the time against a random bot, while it could not beat a scripted bot" — not a single game.

**Bot Bowl III (2021).** MimicBot (Pezzotti) won — the only ML win in the competition's history. GrodBot 2nd, Dryad (MCTS with forward model) 3rd. MimicBot is a hybrid: behavioural cloning of the FRAMEWORK'S SCRIPTED BOT (400k state-action pairs from just 200 scripted-vs-scripted games), then A2C fine-tuning, wrapped in scripted guardrails — block-die choice is scripted, re-rolls auto-used to avoid turnovers, blocks with >70% turnover chance masked out of the action space, ball-carrier forced to block last, kick-off placement scripted. The paper (arXiv:2108.09478) is candid: curriculum RL previously "fail[ed] to consistently beat a randomly playing agent" and "no machine learning approach can beat a scripted bot." MimicBot's final numbers vs the scripted baseline (which "simulates a novice human player"): 188/77/35 in 300 games — 62.7% wins, 11.7% losses. Pure behavioural cloning alone LOST to its own teacher (61/99/140). So state of the art in 2021 = beats a novice-level scripted bot ~63% of the time, and only with the scripted bot as teacher plus scripted guardrails.

**Bot Bowl IV (2022) and V (2023).** Both won by Drefsante (Frederic Bair) — a SCRIPTED bot by a single hobbyist. ML prizes went to MotBot (neural nets + engineered features + evolutionary algorithms; reported via Wargamer) and Treeman (Busk's MCTS line). Wargamer reports (I could not verify against a primary results table, which the official pages no longer expose) that in Bot Bowl IV the RANDOM baseline collected seven draws, including one against the winner Drefsante. Drefsante's own author wrote of his win: "number of playing games during the competition is wayyyyy to low to be significant enough to see which AI/Bot is the better" — 10 games per pairing was statistically meaningless, a problem your lab has already quantified in your own domain.

**After 2023: nothing.** The botbowl repo's last commit is 2023-08-25 ("Update bot-bowl-v.md"); there was no Bot Bowl VI in 2024 or 2025 that I can find any trace of. Prize pool shrank from $1,000 (2019) to $500 total (2023). The README's stated aim — "develop a bot that can beat the best humans at the game" — was never approached. Final scoreboard over five competitions: scripted 4, hybrid-ML-built-on-scripted 1, pure ML 0, search 0.

## Verifying your memory ("bots still lost to random")

Close but not literal. I found no instance of a bot LOSING a game to the random baseline (random scored 0 TDs across every table I saw; it cannot win). The true, verified versions of your memory: (1) Bot Bowl I's A2C entrant finished level with Random (0 wins each); (2) Bot Bowl II's Giaobot finished BELOW Random in the standings; (3) as late as 2022, the tournament WINNER conceded a draw to the random baseline (reported). The draws-with-random pathology is a direct artifact of Blood Bowl's sparse scoring: a random opponent can 0-0 you because scoring requires ~5-10 correct sequential dice-gated steps. That artifact does not exist in a game where standing near an objective scores every round.

## Why learning failed there (organizers' and winners' own words)

Verified causes, stated in the papers: (a) reward sparsity — 350k random games, zero TDs, so exploration never finds the signal without heavy shaping; (b) huge, uneven, state-dependent action space (8,117 discrete actions in MimicBot's flattened encoding; 1-395 legal at any step) — "the large and uneven number of actions available"; (c) intrinsic randomness — turnover makes single dice rolls catastrophic, so returns are extremely high-variance; (d) nested multi-action turns — credit assignment across ~42 actions/turn; (e) forward-model economics — FFAI steps at ~0.9 ms and plays a full random game in 0.16 s, but the object-oriented state is slow to clone, and the 2019 paper flat-out says tree search is "possible, but not very feasible." The MCTS lineage confirms it: Sapling went 1/20/39 in 2020 (barely above random), and it took Busk three more years to get an MCTS bot to an ML prize with no title.

## Humans

There is NO bot-vs-human competition data. Bot Bowl never staged human matches; FUMBBL (2.5M+ recorded human games) keeps its client source secret to prevent cheating and never integrated bots into ranked play; the framework only mined it for replays. The only calibration anchors, both from the authors: GrodBot has "an estimated skill level slightly higher than a rookie player" (Justesen 2019), and the FFAI scripted bot "simulates a novice human player" (Pezzotti 2021). My inference: after five years, SOTA Blood Bowl AI was plausibly still below a decent club player, and nobody ever measured it because it wasn't close enough to be interesting. The commercial Cyanide video game AI is described in the 2019 paper as "far from human-level."

## LLMs

Nothing in the official botbowl ecosystem ever used LLMs (verified by absence across papers.md, the repo, and searches). The only related artifact is a 2025 hobby project (Varotsis, "Ankh-Morpork Scramble"): ten small open-weight LLMs playing a simplified Blood Bowl clone where the server enumerates legal moves as natural language. Result: locally sensible play (pick up ball, protect carrier, retreat from bad spots), planning limited to ~1-2 turns, and it only worked at all once the action space was constrained server-side. No benchmark vs scripted bots, no competitive claim.

## Transfer analysis: what carries to 40k and what does not

**Transfers (bad news).** (1) Scripted domain knowledge beats generic ML at hobby compute — 4 of 5 titles, and the fifth needed the scripted bot as teacher plus guardrails. (2) Parameter-tuning a mature heuristic yields ~nothing: EvoGrod (evolved params) lost to hand-tuned GrodBot; your CEM over 238 params found +2.25 +/- 3.2 VP. Two independent replications of the same null. (3) Evaluation noise throttles progress: 10-game pairings were meaningless in Bot Bowl; your sd-11-20-VP margins need hundreds of paired games. (4) Nested multi-turn structure defeats greedy one-ply scoring — GrodBot's flagged weaknesses (setups, long-term planning) are exactly your movement pathology (50% dominated destinations). (5) Search is compute-gated: FFAI's forward model is roughly THREE orDERS of magnitude faster than your engine (0.16 s vs 1-8 min per game) and search still underperformed there for years. Rollout-based lookahead is dead on arrival for you. (6) The proven lever in both games is hand-authored strategic structure: formations/cages in Blood Bowl were authored, not learned (even the random agent uses predefined formations); your one big win was strategic (reserves cap, 12->39 VP).

**Does NOT transfer (genuinely good news).** (1) No turnover: 40k has no mechanic where one failed roll ends your turn. Dice aggregate over dozens of attacks per phase, so EV approximations are reliable and per-decision variance is forgiving; Blood Bowl's core skill (risk sequencing under catastrophic single-roll failure) simply isn't the core 40k skill. (2) Dense reward: 40k scores primary VP every battle round for standing on objectives; random play still scores; your own lab already measures smooth VP gradients. Blood Bowl's root cause of RL failure — 350k games, zero reward — is ABSENT. (3) The draw-with-random pathology cannot recur: a competent 40k bot should beat random play by enormous VP margins every game (worth verifying with a random-baseline agent — Bot Bowl teaches you to keep that floor anchor in the lab). (4) The bar is different: Bot Bowl chased "beat the best humans" in a game with a world-championship scene and died trying; you need "challenging for one human" in your own implementation. Blood Bowl evidence says a single part-time developer's scripted bot reaches "slightly above rookie human" in a MUCH less forgiving game. With dense scoring, no turnover, and authored deployment/mission plans, a scripted 40k bot punishing casual mistakes is a materially easier target than anything Bot Bowl attempted.

## Implications

Bot Bowl is NOT evidence your project is infeasible — but it is strong evidence about WHICH paths are dead. (1) Your current architecture (scripted heuristic core + hand-authored strategy) is exactly the architecture that won 4 of 5 Bot Bowls, and the one ML winner needed that scripted core as teacher plus guardrails; keep it. (2) Both Bot Bowl (EvoGrod losing to GrodBot) and your CEM run show parameter optimization of a mature heuristic returns ~zero — stop spending compute there; wins in both games came from structural/strategic changes (formations/cages there; your reserves-cap 12->39 VP here). Your proposed pre-authored deployment plans and mission/army game plans are precisely the Blood Bowl-proven lever, and deployment was the scripted bots' flagged weakness there too. (3) Forget rollout search: FFAI simulated a full game in 0.16s and MCTS still floundered for years; your engine at 1-8 min/game is ~3 orders of magnitude too slow. If you ever want lookahead, it must be a stripped abstract state evaluator (unit-level, no per-model sim), not game rollouts. (4) The nightmare scenario ('bot draws/loses to random') was caused by turnover + zero-reward sparsity, which 40k lacks — but add a random-policy agent and a 'sit on objectives, never shoot' agent as permanent floor baselines in your lab; if your AI doesn't beat both by huge margins every game, that is the Blood Bowl red flag. (5) Calibrate the goal: Bot Bowl died chasing 'beat the best humans'; a one-developer scripted bot reached 'slightly above rookie human' in a far less forgiving game. 'Challenging for a human' in your own game — where you also control mission/terrain complexity — is a materially lower bar, and dense VP scoring means your opponent-punishing heuristics actually get graded every round. (6) LLM-per-decision play has zero supporting evidence and prohibitive latency; LLM-at-design-time (authoring plans) is untested by Bot Bowl, and your own transcript-mining null (-2.05 VP) says knowledge injection must go through your measurement loop. Verdict: 'not worth pursuing' is not supported; 'not worth pursuing via learning or search' largely is. The feasible target is a scripted AI with authored deployment/mission plans, validated by your paired-seed evaluator at sample sizes Bot Bowl never had.
