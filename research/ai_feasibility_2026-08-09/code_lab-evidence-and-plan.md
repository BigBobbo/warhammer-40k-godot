## 1. The remaining plan and its cost

The plan (`.llm/ai-overhaul-todo.md`) is 37 tasks in six workstreams. Per the progress journal (`.llm/ai-overhaul-progress.md:145-152`), Phase 1 is complete except B1; Phases 2-5 are untouched. Done: A1-A5, A7 (instrumentation/reachability), B0, B2, B4 (frozen baseline, asym fixture, exams), D1 (combat-math oracle), D1b melee half. Open: 26-28 items.

Remaining costs as the plan itself states them: A6b ~6 games; B1 profiling + ~20; B3 Ork sensitivity screen ~300 (todo:750, "else ~28h" without B1); B5 nightly runner time; B6 3-day spike; WS-C: C0 code-only, C1 ~100-game season, C2 ~150, C2b ~200, C3/C4 ~100 each, C5 ~150, C6/C7 ~100 each (todo:888-1109); WS-D: D1b ranged ~100/class, D2 microbenchmarks, D3 ~200, D4 ~150 (todo:1209-1347); WS-E: E1 **1,500-3,500 games, "2-4 days unattended after B1"** (todo:1358), E2 ~300/cycle and E3 ~400/cycle with 3-cycle kill criteria (todo:1376-1430); WS-F: F1 ~100, F2 ~200, F3 ~150. Summing fixed budgets ≈ 2,200 games, plus E1's 1,500-3,500, plus up to ~2,100 for E2/E3 cycles: **roughly 4,000-8,000 games total**. At the new 2000-pt fixture costs (194 s/game Custodes predeploy measured, todo:87-92; Ork fixture "materially worse" with 77 models/side; old 1840-pt Ork mirror was ~487 s), that is on the order of **1-3 weeks of continuous 4-core compute**, before authoring time — and B1, the task meant to make games cheap, had its premise disproved by measurement: a 20x time_scale change moved wall clock <10% (todo:615-651); the real levers (~15 s/game process overhead, ~115 ms/AI action, hot path unprofiled) remain unfixed.

**Dependency chain risk**: half the remaining plan hangs on C1, an unbuilt board-evaluation function with a falsifiable gate (round-3 V vs final margin Spearman ≥0.5, kill at <0.35, todo:924-934). C2←C1, C2b←C1+C2, C5←C2b, D3←C1+D2, F1←C2b. The layer the evidence says matters most (deployment/reserves, C2b/C5) is at the END of this chain, behind ~450 games of gates.

**What the measured evidence weakens**: (a) **WS-D lookahead** — D3/D4 score reply damage through the AI's expected-damage math. Two sizeable corrections to that math measured ~zero: D1's 1.6-3.0x melee wound-overflow fix pooled E=−0.74±0.75 (todo:1188-1197), and D1b's melee-keyword fix was a **provable no-op on the Ork mirror (every paired seed identical)**, +0.28±0.28 on Custodes (todo:1255-1262). The progress journal itself names the pattern: "the tactical half of the plan may be pulling on a rope that is not attached" and recommends a deliberate degraded-arm test (scale expected-damage ×0.7/×1.4) **before** WS-D spends games (progress:190-199, 275-283). (b) The sensitivity screen found 13/21 parameters — every charge, fight and shooting coefficient tested — changed **zero decisions** at ±30% (2026-08-07_m3_sensitivity_screen.md:19-31). (c) **E1's rationale is weakened but not dead**: the 264-game CEM null (+2.25±3.2) ran at a tenth of design budget on a space missing its most influential member (MOVE_REACHABLE_BONUS, |E|=9.25, hardcoded until that morning — screen report:38-44); reachability is now 78%→42% unreachable. But the B2 correction (todo:726-747) concedes every number ever measured comes from **two factions**, so even a gated E1 win reads as "better at Custodes-vs-Orks", and the Ork fixture packing defect (0.07" gaps grinding the mover, progress:118-133) requires a rebuild that invalidates the A/A tables. (d) **F2 personas** would be authored from YouTube-derived draft profiles whose only measured implementation scored −2.05 VP.

## 2. Evidence about strength vs humans: none

I searched ISSUES.md (a migration tracker — no player feedback), version_history.json, docs, and all baselines. **There is not a single human-vs-AI game record, difficulty-usage statistic, or piece of player feedback about AI strength anywhere in the repo.** The only human-adjacent artifacts are UI plumbing (AI Suggestion button, AI speed presets, pass-the-device). Version history makes an unmeasured claim ("Normal difficulty… should be noticeably more challenging out of the box", version_history.json:4485). Indirect evidence a human would exploit: 49.6% of movement decisions chose destinations both farther and lower-scoring than an alternative (audit_findings:60-62); the AI declares 1-in-36 hopeless charges (exam ch01, todo:817-821); doesn't prioritize finishing a 1-wound character over a fresh squad (sh01, todo:821); Tier-0 bugs like "D6"→1.0 undervaluing every anti-tank gun 2-3.5x shipped for months (AUDIT_AI_RULES.md:50); difficulty below Hard silently disables cross-phase planning (todo:879-881) and is otherwise score noise with 7 dead config gates (todo:1546-1551). Whether the current AI beats, challenges, or amuses a competent human is **completely unmeasured**.

## 3. What the YouTube corpus says strong play is, vs what the AI does

31 findings from 1,029 transcripts/10.5M words (research/youtube/README.md, ai_learnings.json). Top principles: **Hidden/concealment positioning** (F001/F002/F005 — end moves in dense terrain, don't shoot chip shots that forfeit Hidden); **Overwatch tax on movement** (F004); **screening geometry** — gaps relative to enemy base size (F006), deep-strike denial at the arriver's own distance (F007), screens must survive into the opponent's turn vs Rapid Ingress (F029); **kill screens first, above their points value** (F008); **battle-shock to flip objectives** (F009); **focus fire until dead** (F010); **trade units, don't chase tabling** (F011); **reserves as one reactive unit, not the army** (F013); **deployment robust to being forced first turn** (F019); **Waaagh!/ability timing** (F020); **objective-holders are premium targets every round** (F028). Six were implemented (F001-F004, F007, F009 — 2026-08-06_youtube_findings_ab.md:7-16); measured **E=−2.05 VP, CI [−7.2,+3.1]** (2026-08-06_mirror_new_vs_old.md:76-78), after which the five behavioural weights were defaulted to neutral (shipped OFF) and only the bug-fix class kept (mirror_new_vs_old:144-161). F006 was deliberately not implemented (10e rule). Focus-fire, Waaagh! timing, objective-holder premium, and a 25% reserves cap (COORD-6) exist natively; screening exists but is only offered to units left over after objective assignment (progress:84-89). Two corpus admissions matter: **deployment "is discussed constantly but almost always in terms of a specific board… does not generalise into a weight. No deployment finding was safe to emit"** and secondary discard "is barely reasoned about on camera" (ai_learnings.json gaps) — the corpus is weakest exactly where the lab says the game is decided.

`research/ai_learning_framework_design.md` (stage M5 "rule search and LLM-proposed rules") is **referenced everywhere but absent from the repo** — only the commissioning prompt (`research/ai_learning_framework_prompt.md`, whose RQ2 asks "where does an LLM genuinely help, and where is it theatre?", lines 161-166) and the harness overview survive. The overview's final stage: rule-DSL search is "the one place an LLM plausibly beats its cheap baseline — proposing semantically sensible rules random mutation would take thousands of games to stumble on", kill criterion "dies if its accept-rate per 1k games doesn't beat templated mutation". ## Implications

For the go/no-go verdict: (1) The question "is the AI challenging for humans?" has literally no data behind it — before any of the 4,000-8,000-game plan is funded, a handful of human-vs-AI games would be the cheapest, highest-information experiment in the entire project, and nothing in the plan schedules one. (2) The measured record is brutally consistent: five independent plausible improvements (YouTube findings, scoring horizon, CEM search, two damage-math corrections) all measured at or below zero on a verified instrument, while the only levers showing influence are macro movement/objective weights and pregame decisions (reserves, deployment) — the latter resting on 2-game directional evidence and costing 3-4x more to measure. This supports the user's instinct toward scripted/pre-authored deployment and game plans: the evidence says improvement lives in the strategic layer, which is exactly what pre-authored plans target at near-zero games cost, versus WS-D lookahead which the repo's own data argues is attached to a rope the AI barely pulls. (3) The plan is honest and well-gated but long: its highest-value remaining items by its own evidence are the cheap hypothesis tests (degraded-damage arm, ~4 hours) and C1-dark, not the expensive tail (D3/D4, E1-E3). Any continuation decision should treat the 3-cycle kill criteria and C1's correlation gate as real stop conditions, note that B1's failure makes every downstream games estimate optimistic, and weigh that even total success is currently only provable on one faction matchup. (4) LLM leverage is designed but unproven here; the repo's own analysis says its unique value is proposing rule-DSL hypotheses and naming missing concepts, not tuning numbers — and warns that anything a grep can find, an LLM finding it is theatre.
